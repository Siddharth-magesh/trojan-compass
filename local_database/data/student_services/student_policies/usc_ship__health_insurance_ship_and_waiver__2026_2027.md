# USC health insurance requirement, SHIP/Aetna and the waiver (2026-2027)

## Health insurance requirement and automatic SHIP/Aetna enrollment

USC requires every student to have health insurance meeting minimum standards. International students, PhD students and health-professions (HSC) students are automatically enrolled in the Student Health Insurance Plan (SHIP, provided through Aetna Student Health) regardless of units; domestic students are auto-enrolled at 6+ units. The premium is charged to your student account and shows on your eBill in USCe.pay. Insurance covers care outside Student Health: hospitalization, surgery, emergency, specialists, prescriptions, longer-term therapy, urgent care, vision (VSP). Dental is not included; an optional Delta Dental PPO plan is available with separate enrollment. The SHIP plan year is 365 days, from mid-August.

During fall and spring semesters you need a Student Health referral before seeing an outside provider (except emergencies); during breaks the referral rule is waived and it works like a nationwide PPO. Coverage tiers: Tier 1 USC-designated providers (Keck Medicine of USC, Children's Hospital LA, USC Verdugo Hills, Good Samaritan, USC Norris and others) 90%; Tier 2 Aetna network 80%; Tier 3 out-of-network 50%. Copays: $20 medical visit and $10 mental health visit with Tier 1/2 providers; $50 urgent care walk-in. Find in-network providers: Aetna Student Health 877-626-2299.

## Can an international student waive SHIP? Waiver requirements and how to submit

You may waive SHIP only if your own plan meets USC's criteria; most international (home-country) plans do not. General criteria: Minimum Essential Coverage, with unrestricted access to an in-network primary care provider, in-network hospital, and full non-emergency medical and behavioral health care reasonably close to campus (recommended within 50 miles). Extra requirements for international students: meets U.S. Department of State visa requirements; written in standard English with all benefits in U.S. dollars; a U.S.-based claims office that pays U.S. providers directly; at least $50,000 per year medical evacuation; at least $25,000 repatriation of remains.

Common denial reasons: emergency-only coverage in the area, regional HMO/EPO with no LA network, out-of-state Medicaid (not accepted for on-campus residents), and healthcare sharing programs. How to waive: confirm your plan qualifies, submit the waiver in MySHR (Insurance Waiver/Enrollment tab) or via Aetna's USC waiver page (https://www33.aetna.com/ASH.SSO/USC) before the Friday of the third week of classes, wait for approval, and repeat every year. Spring starters submit by the third week of spring, then each fall. If you do nothing, you stay on SHIP and the premium remains on your account.

Source page: https://sites.usc.edu/new-student-health-requirements/costs/insurance-waiver-requirements/

## How to get your Aetna (SHIP) insurance card

SHIP/Aetna members print or download their card from the Aetna Student Health member portal (https://www.aetnastudenthealth.com/en/school/474947/members/get-id-card.html) or use the Aetna Health app for a digital card. The card is available at the start of the plan year (mid-August) after you register for classes; Student Health advises printing it on or after the first day of classes and keeping a photo on your phone. Bring it whenever you get care outside Student Health, e.g., the pharmacy, labs or urgent care. Coverage works across the U.S., including travel and trips home within the U.S.

## Student Health Fee (SHF) vs insurance: what is free at Student Health

The Student Health Fee is charged every semester on your student account (visible in USCe.pay) and cannot be waived; it is separate from the insurance premium. It funds medical care, mental health services, immunizations, sexual assault survivor support, prevention education, medical records and the 24/7 phone line. Medical and counseling visits at the campus health centers, workshops, group therapy and prevention programs have no copays and are not billed to insurance. Possible extra charges (billed to insurance where applicable): some lab tests, radiology, immunizations, and equipment such as crutches or boots.

Services outside Student Health (e.g., Psychiatry and Behavioral Health, the private practice on Engemann's 5th floor, pharmacy prescriptions) may need copays. Online-only students are excluded from the fee.
